# SELECT and SOUL: What the Two Semaglutide Cardiovascular Trials Found, and What They Left Open

Two placebo-controlled outcome trials tested whether semaglutide prevents cardiovascular events, and both succeeded. SELECT gave subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly to 17,604 adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity but no diabetes, and cut the three-point MACE composite from 8.0% to 6.5% (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90; P<0.001) [1][2]. SOUL gave once-daily oral semaglutide, escalated to 14 mg, to 9,650 adults with type 2 diabetes plus atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and/or chronic kidney disease, and cut the same composite from 13.8% to 12.0% (HR 0.86, 0.77–0.96; P=0.006) [3][4].

The two trials are routinely placed side by side, but they answer different questions and cannot be used to rank the injection against the tablet. Neither primary publication reported an absolute risk reduction or a number needed to treat; the familiar "~1.5 percentage points, NNT 67" and "~1.8 points, NNT 56" figures are arithmetic performed on the published event rates. SELECT's secondary and safety results are documented in detail, SOUL's are only partly so, and only SELECT has been written into a product label.

## What SELECT showed

### Design and population

SELECT randomised 17,604 participants at 804 sites in 41 countries between October 2018 and March 2021, with a target of 1,225 primary events and 90% power to detect a 17% relative reduction [1]. Entry required age ≥45, BMI ≥27 and prior myocardial infarction, stroke or symptomatic peripheral arterial disease, with diabetes excluded by HbA1c and by a ban on glucose-lowering drugs or GLP-1 receptor agonists in the preceding 90 days [1]. The label's baseline table records prior MI in 76%, prior stroke in 23%, peripheral arterial disease in 9% and heart failure in 24%; 84% of participants were White and 72% male, with a mean age of 62 years and mean BMI 33 [5]. Background therapy was intensive: lipid-lowering drugs in 90.1%, antiplatelet agents in 86.2%, beta-blockers in 70.2% [1].

### Primary result and how large the absolute benefit is

MACE occurred in 569 of 8,803 participants (6.5%) on semaglutide and 701 of 8,801 (8.0%) on placebo, a hazard ratio of 0.80 (0.72–0.90) [1]. The paper reports no absolute risk reduction and no number needed to treat, and neither does the Wegovy label [1][2]. Subtracting the event rates gives about 1.5 percentage points over a mean 39.8 months, or one event avoided per roughly 67 patients treated for that period — a derivation from the published rates, not a trial-reported figure, and one that ignores censoring.

### Secondary outcomes and safety

The cardiovascular-mortality result is the important negative. CV death occurred in 2.5% versus 3.0%, HR 0.85 (0.71–1.01), which failed the hierarchical testing gate; the label states that superiority for CV death was "not confirmed" [1][2]. All-cause death fell from 5.2% to 4.3%, HR 0.81 (0.71–0.93) [1][2]. A heart-failure composite occurred in 3.4% versus 4.1%, HR 0.82 (0.71–0.96), but sat outside the formal hierarchy and the label states that an effect on heart failure "has not been established" [1][2]. Tolerability carried a real cost: permanent discontinuation for adverse events was 16.6% versus 8.2%, with gastrointestinal disorders in 10.0% versus 2.0% [1].

### A weight-independent signal

A prespecified adiposity analysis found that adjusting for time-varying weight loss did not attenuate the effect — unadjusted HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90) versus adjusted 0.81 (0.71–0.93) — that early waist-circumference change marked about 33% of the benefit, and that the MACE reduction was already present before week 20 (HR 0.58, 0.41–0.81) [6]. These are model-based mediation estimates on randomised trial data, not a measured mechanism, and the label states plainly that the mechanism of cardiovascular risk reduction has not been established [2].

## What SOUL showed

### Primary result in a higher-risk diabetes population

SOUL randomised 9,650 participants across 444 sites in 33 countries from June 2019 to March 2021, with oral semaglutide titrated from 3 mg to 7 mg to 14 mg daily against placebo, both on standard care [3][4]. The population had long-standing, poorly controlled diabetes: mean HbA1c 8.0%, diabetes duration 15.4 years, insulin in 51%, metformin in 76%, SGLT2 inhibitor in 27%, established cardiovascular disease in 84%, prior heart failure in 23%, statin use about 75% [7]. Mean age was 66 and 29% were female [7].

MACE occurred in 579 of 4,825 (12.0%, 3.1 per 100 person-years) versus 668 of 4,825 (13.8%, 3.7 per 100 person-years), HR 0.86 (0.77–0.96) [3][4]. As with SELECT, no ARR or NNT appears in the paper; the crude difference of 1.8 percentage points implies about 56 patients treated over the median 49.5 months per event avoided.

One caveat about secondary summaries: at least one clinical-trial summary page describes SOUL's primary endpoint as all-cause death, nonfatal MI or nonfatal stroke. The journal record and the prespecified Circulation analysis both define it as cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI or nonfatal stroke, and that is the definition to use [3][4].

### Subgroups

Two prespecified analyses add texture. By baseline SGLT2-inhibitor use the effect was consistent — HR 0.89 (0.71–1.11) with and 0.84 (0.74–0.95) without, interaction P=0.66 — although in-trial SGLT2-inhibitor initiation was far higher on placebo (35.5% versus 24.7%, HR 0.63), so that comparison is not randomised [4]. By extent of vascular disease the point estimates were 0.59 (0.39–0.88) in participants with no known atherosclerotic disease, 0.87 (0.74–1.02) with isolated disease and 0.92 (0.75–1.13) with polyvascular disease (interaction P=0.15), with 3-year NNTs of 38, 58 and 55 respectively [8]. These subgroups are exploratory and underpowered; the apparent advantage in the lowest-risk stratum should not be read as a proven gradient.

### Secondary outcomes and safety are only partly documented

The SOUL record available here is the journal's abstract page, without the full results tables. It reports serious adverse events in 47.9% versus 50.3% and gastrointestinal disorders in 5.0% versus 4.4% [3]. The five-point major kidney disease composite "did not differ significantly between the two groups," with no hazard ratio or confidence interval given [3]. Cardiovascular death, all-cause death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke, heart-failure hospitalisation and gastrointestinal discontinuation are absent from that record. The 5.0% versus 4.4% gastrointestinal figure is not comparable with SELECT's 10.0% versus 2.0%, because the two abstracts report them in different contexts.

A separate post hoc analysis quantified risk-factor changes rather than outcomes: at week 156, HbA1c was 0.47 percentage points lower and body weight 3.26 percentage points lower with oral semaglutide [9].

## Why the two hazard ratios differ — and why the trials cannot settle it

The hazard ratios (0.80 versus 0.86) invite a formulation comparison that the data do not support. Four facts block it:

- **The populations barely overlap.** SELECT excluded diabetes entirely and enrolled stable secondary prevention with 90% statin use; SOUL enrolled 15-year diabetes with HbA1c 8.0%, half on insulin, a quarter with prior heart failure and only about three-quarters on statins [1][7].
- **The confidence intervals overlap substantially** (0.72–0.90 versus 0.77–0.96), so the difference in point estimates is not statistically distinguishable from noise [1][3].
- **No randomised head-to-head exists,** and the two trials differ simultaneously in population, formulation, dose, trial era and background therapy.
- **The one analysis that formally tested route of administration excludes SELECT.** A meta-analysis of ten placebo-controlled GLP-1 receptor agonist trials in type 2 diabetes (n=71,351, including SOUL and FLOW but not SELECT, which enrolled people without diabetes) found a pooled MACE HR of 0.86 (0.81–0.90) with no heterogeneity by subcutaneous versus oral route (interaction P=0.98) [7]. That answers a question about oral versus injectable semaglutide within diabetes, not the SELECT-versus-SOUL question.

The more informative comparison is absolute, not relative. SELECT's placebo arm ran at 2.5 events per 100 person-years after week 20 versus 2.0 on semaglutide; SOUL's ran at 3.7 versus 3.1 [3][6]. Calculated from those rates, the two trials prevented a similar number of events per year of treatment — roughly one per 220 to 230 patient-years — even though SELECT's relative reduction was larger. Expressed per event avoided over each trial's own follow-up, the derived NNTs of about 67 and 56 sit on different time scales (3.3 versus 4.1 years) and should not be quoted as if they were directly comparable.

## Regulatory labelling and guidelines

Translation into labelling has been one-sided. The FDA granted Wegovy a cardiovascular indication for reducing MACE in adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity [10]. The current label carries that indication for both the 2.4 mg subcutaneous injection and a 25 mg oral tablet, drawing on SELECT (Study 1, NCT03574597, median follow-up 41.8 months, HR 0.80 [0.72–0.90]), and explicitly flags that CV-death superiority was not confirmed, that a heart-failure effect has not been established, and that the mechanism is unknown [2].

The Ozempic injection label does not draw on either trial: its cardiovascular indication rests on SUSTAIN-6 (HR 0.74, 0.58–0.95) and its kidney indication on FLOW (HR 0.76, 0.66–0.88) [11]. This report did not find a regulatory document incorporating SOUL into any US label; a promotional vendor page asserts an oral-semaglutide label expansion in October 2025, and that claim could not be verified against a label text. No guideline document from ADA, ESC or ACC/AHA was retrieved, so the class and level of evidence assigned to semaglutide for cardiovascular risk reduction after these trials remains unconfirmed here.

## What remains open

**SOUL's secondary and safety tables.** Component-level MACE, cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, heart-failure hospitalisation, the kidney composite's effect estimate and gastrointestinal discontinuation rates are all missing from the accessible record [3]. Until they are published in full, SOUL cannot be compared with SELECT on safety, and the kidney question depends on a prespecified pooled analysis of SELECT, FLOW and SOUL whose results were not accessible [3].

**Mechanism.** SELECT's benefit appeared within 20 weeks and survived adjustment for weight loss [6], while the label disclaims any established mechanism [2]. Whether the effect is driven by plaque stabilisation, inflammation, blood pressure, glycaemia or something else is unresolved; an observational electronic-record study finding that benefit tracked attained dose rather than achieved weight loss is hypothesis-generating, not causal [12].

**Oral versus injectable.** No randomised comparison, and no pooled analysis that includes SELECT, exists to test whether the two formulations produce equivalent cardiovascular protection [7].

**Generalisability.** SELECT was 72% male and 84% White [5]; SOUL was 71% male [7]. Effects in women, in younger adults, in non-White populations and in primary prevention without established disease are not established by either trial.

**Durability.** Neither trial reports follow-up beyond its randomised period. Whether the benefit persists, attenuates or rebounds after stopping treatment is unknown.

**Cost-effectiveness.** No economic evaluation of either trial's strategy was found, so the cost per event avoided implied by a roughly 1.5–1.8 percentage-point absolute reduction over four years has not been formally assessed.

**Guideline positioning.** How professional societies grade semaglutide for cardiovascular risk reduction in people with and without diabetes, and whether SOUL changes any recommendation for oral semaglutide, could not be determined.
